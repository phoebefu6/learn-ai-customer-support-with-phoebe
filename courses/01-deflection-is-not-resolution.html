Real world

Gartner surveyed 5,728 customers in December 2023 and reported in July 2024 that 64 percent "would prefer that companies didn't use artificial intelligence (AI) in their customer service". The top concern was that "it will get more difficult reaching a person". That is the customer's version of the question above, and it is why a support plan has to answer it in numbers.

Part 1 · covers containment, deflection, resolution, repeat contact

Four words that sound the same and count different things

7 min live

Containment counts where the chat ended. Resolution counts whether the problem ended. Most of what goes wrong with support bots lives in the gap between those two sentences, and the gap has a name: false containment.

LiveThe four words, defined so nobody can move them4 min▶
WordWhat it countsWhen you can count it
ContainmentConversations that ended without reaching a personThe moment the chat closes
DeflectionContacts that never reached a person because something else answered first. Vendors use it loosely, often as a synonym for containmentThe moment the chat closes
ResolutionCustomers whose problem actually endedOnly after a window, and only with a check
Repeat contactThe same customer, about the same issue, inside the windowAt the end of the window

False containment is the difference: a conversation counted as contained whose problem was not resolved. Every one of them is a customer who was told, in effect, that they were done.

The one-line test. Ask of any number on a vendor slide: could it have been computed the moment the chat ended? If yes, it is a containment number, whatever the slide calls it.

Self-studyThere is no standard window, so choose one and write it down4 min▶

The contact-centre research firm SQM Group defines internal first contact resolution by whether the customer came back "for the same issue within 1 to 30 days", and adds that "there is no standard for internal FCR measurement". A poll reported by Call Centre Helper found "the most common method of measuring FCR was to track repeat contacts within a given timeframe".

So the window is a decision, not a fact. Quayside picks 7 days: long enough to see a transfer that never arrived or a refund that never showed, short enough to report weekly. A bank with monthly statements might reasonably pick 30. What is not reasonable is leaving the choice to whoever builds the dashboard.

Real world

Klarna's February 2024 announcement of its AI assistant listed many numbers. One of them was "a 25% drop in repeat inquiries". Session 5 takes the whole announcement apart; for now, notice that the repeat number is the only one in the list that speaks to resolution rather than to where the chat ended.

Part 2 · covers how vendors count a resolution

Why the dashboard reports the flattering number

6 min live

The vendor is not lying. It is counting what it can see from where it sits, which is the end of the chat. Resolution needs two things the bot cannot see: what the customer did next, and whether the answer was right.

LiveWhat three vendors say they count, in their own words4 min▶

Each of these was read on the vendor's own page on 2026-11-09. Pricing and definitions here change often: re-verify before you put any of it in a contract.

Intercom Fin, help centre: what counts as a resolution A resolution is counted when, following Fin's last answer in a conversation, the customer confirms ("Ok thanks", "That helped") "or exits the conversation without requesting further assistance (assumed resolution)."

Intercom's pricing page lists Fin at "$0.99 per outcome", and the FAQ counts the case where customers "don't ask for more help after Fin responds". An assumed resolution is billed like a confirmed one. If the customer returns to the same conversation, "that resolution will be deducted and not charged". A new conversation three days later is a different conversation.

★ Zendesk, resolution tiers introduced May 18, 2026 Contained: "The AI agent handled the interaction to completion without the customer requesting further assistance." "This tier does not count against your resolution allowance." Verified: "The AI agent successfully resolved the interaction." A large language model "evaluates the text of the conversation to confirm that the customer's request was satisfactorily resolved."

Zendesk's windows: email ends "72 hours after the first email", messaging "2 hours after the first message" by default (it "can be increased up to 72 hours"). Its own product now separates contained from verified, which is the distinction this session is about, drawn by a vendor.

Salesforce lists Agentforce at "$2" per conversation on its pricing page, with Flex Credits as the newer model. No Salesforce page that defines a conversation, containment or deflection could be read, so none is quoted here.

Self-studyWhere the definition gets negotiated, and who owns that conversation3 min▶

Whether a vendor's definition of resolution is good enough to launch on, and how to write the resolution-definition clause into the contract, belongs to the launch gate. That is taught in full in Agent Launch: gate the chatbot ↗. This session takes over after launch: the operating number you report every week, whatever the contract says.

A support-QA vendor put the problem in one sentence: "Containment counts the absence of a human, not the presence of a resolution." It is a vendor blog, so treat it as a quotable line rather than evidence, but it is the right line.

Part 3 · covers what false containment costs

Every false containment shows up somewhere else on the P&L

4 min live

A falsely contained customer does not disappear. Some come back angrier and take longer to serve. Some do not come back, and some of those leave. The bot's cost line looks better and the cost moves to lines nobody connected to the bot.

LiveWhere the five falsely contained customers go4 min▶

Take the 5 false containments from the figure in Part 1. In Quayside's constructed model, which session 6 prices, three things happen:

  • Most come back. The model assumes 60 percent return within the window, and each repeat takes 1.5 times the normal handle time, because the customer explains the problem again and is less patient doing it.
  • The rest go quiet. They are invisible to every containment number and to the repeat-contact number too. Only reading a sample finds them, which is why session 5 exists.
  • Some of the quiet ones leave. The model assumes 15 percent of them close their account. Churn modelling itself is a different discipline, taught in Customer Retention: churn prediction ↗; here it is one honest line on the support P&L.

Every one of those percentages is constructed, and the bench labels them so. What is not constructed is the shape: you also paid the AI fee on every falsely contained conversation, because under an assumed-resolution definition they were billed as outcomes.

Self-studyWhat customers say they fear, and what they say they would do3 min▶

The same July 2024 Gartner release reported that "53% of customers would consider switching to a competitor if they found out a company was going to use AI for customer service". It is a stated intention from a survey, not observed behaviour, so do not put it in a forecast. It is still the right thing to read aloud when someone proposes measuring the bot by containment alone: the thing customers say they fear is exactly the thing containment cannot see.

Constructed Quayside example. Rows 14 and 17 hide something the log cannot show: step 3 finds it.

Count containment the vendor's way. Every chat that ended without a person counts. That is every row except 1, 5, 9, 13, 16 and 19: 14 of 20, which is 70 percent. This is the number on the slide.

★ Containment chats that ended without a person / all bot chats = 14 / 20 = 70%

Apply the 7-day window. Rows 3, 7 and 11 came back about the same issue. They were contained and not resolved. Take them out: 11 of 20, which is 55 percent. The repeat-contact window alone removed 15 points.

Read the eleven that look resolved. In a real week you read a sample; here the sample is all eleven. Two fail. Row 14 got the steps for a first-time identity check, not a failing one. Row 17 got instructions for ordering a virtual card, not fixing one. Neither customer came back, and neither problem was solved. True resolution: 9 of 20, which is 45 percent.

★ True resolution (contained, no repeat within 7 days, not failed by the read) / all bot chats = 9 / 20 = 45% False containment = 14 - 9 = 5 of the 14 contained (36%)

Write the definition into the plan. Copy this into section 1 of your own version of Quayside's plan, and change the window if you can defend another one.

★ Plan section 1 · the metric definition Resolved: the customer did not contact us again about the same issue within 7 days, AND the conversation was not failed in the weekly sample read. False containment: ended without a person, and not resolved. We report containment and true resolution every week, side by side. A vendor's "resolution" is reported under the vendor's name, never ours. Owner: the support lead.

Write the weekly line. One sentence that would have stopped the 70 percent slide in its tracks: "Containment 70 percent, true resolution 45 percent, false containment 5 of 14." Two numbers side by side is the whole trick: neither can be quoted without the other.

AI resolution rate: 70%

14 conversations resolved without an agent. 6 escalated. Average handle time under two minutes.

Containment 70%, true resolution 45%.

5 of 14 contained chats were false containment: 3 came back within 7 days, 2 were wrong answers found by reading.

Asks. Row 7: the recipient never got the transfer.

Bot answers. It explains how long transfers normally take.

Chat ends. No reply. Containment counts it, and an assumed-resolution definition bills it.

Day 7. The customer wrote again on day 3. A repeat contact: not resolved.

Read. The read would have caught it anyway: the bot answered the timing question, not the missing money.

Why 45 percent is not a disaster. Six handed-off chats went to people, so they were never the bot's to resolve. Nine customers were genuinely helped without waiting for an agent. The problem is never that the bot resolves fewer than the slide says. It is deciding staffing, cost and customer promises on the slide's number.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · A bot ended 14 of 20 chats without a person. 3 of those customers came back within 7 days about the same issue, and reading the rest finds 2 wrong answers. What is the true resolution rate?

70 percent is containment. 55 percent applies the window but trusts every answer the customer did not complain about. Only 9 customers both stayed away and got a right answer: 45 percent.

2 · Under Intercom's published definition, a customer who leaves the chat after Fin's answer without asking for more help counts as what?

Intercom counts it when the customer "exits the conversation without requesting further assistance (assumed resolution)". A return to the same conversation is deducted; a new conversation days later is not the same conversation.

3 · Why does Quayside's plan report containment and true resolution side by side, rather than replacing one with the other?

Both are true numbers about different things. Reported together, neither can be quoted without the other, and the difference between them is a number with an owner.
